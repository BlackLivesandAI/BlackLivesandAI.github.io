About the collection

Focusing on the United States, this collection brings together scholars examining how AI-related technologies shape, and are shaped by, Black communities. Rather than positioning Black life at the margins of AI scholarship, the volume argues that Black experiences reveal some of the most pressing social, political, and ethical questions confronting artificial intelligence today.

The collection pushes the field forward by developing new theoretical frameworks, examining sector-specific issues across medicine and health, labor, surveillance, education, and other domains, and engaging contemporary debates surrounding community-centered and democratized AI, AI governance, AI ethics, and AI safety. At its core, this volume is concerned not simply with how AI-related technologies affect Black lives, but with the broader question of relationship itself: how Black communities choose to engage these technologies, reshape them in service of collective flourishing, or refuse them when they reproduce exclusion, domination, or harm.

About the editors

An internationally recognized sociologist who studies the social impact of AI and emerging technologies. He is an expert in democratizing AI and community-centric approaches, and regularly publishes research in leading AI journals such as AI & Society, AI and Ethics, and Philosophy and Technology.
